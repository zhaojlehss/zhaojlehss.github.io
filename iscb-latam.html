Integrative Transcriptomic and Structural Bioinformatics Identifies TDO2 as a Druggable Metabolic Target in ER+/HER2− and Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

Jerry Zhao

Introduction

Breast cancer is a heterogeneous malignancy comprising molecularly distinct subtypes with different biological characteristics, therapeutic responses, and clinical outcomes. Estrogen receptor-positive/HER2-negative (ER+/HER2−) breast cancer and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC) represent two major subtypes with distinct molecular profiles. Despite this heterogeneity, breast cancer progression and metastasis may involve shared molecular alterations and therapeutic vulnerabilities. This study integrated transcriptomic and structural bioinformatics approaches to identify TDO2 as a dysregulated metabolic target across ER+/HER2− and TNBC and to evaluate its structural features for drug development.

Materials and Methods

The publicly available ER+/HER2− and TNBC transcriptomic dataset GSE58135 was reanalyzed. Correlation-based analyses were used to assess transcriptional variation among sample groups. Differential expression analyses were performed across tumor, paired adjacent, and normal tissue comparisons, followed by cross-comparison of differentially expressed genes to identify consistently dysregulated targets. Three-dimensional structural analysis was used to characterize structural and functional features of the identified target. Computational antagonist design evaluated candidate molecules based on predicted binding, inhibitory activity, solubility, and potential toxicity.

Results

Cross-comparison of transcriptomic profiles identified TDO2 as a metabolic gene consistently upregulated across all evaluated comparisons. TDO2 expression was elevated in ER+/HER2− and TNBC tumors relative to both normal breast tissue and paired uninvolved adjacent tissue. TDO2 was also elevated in uninvolved tissues adjacent to both tumor subtypes compared with normal breast tissue. TDO2 encodes tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase, which catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step of tryptophan catabolism through the kynurenine pathway, a pathway implicated in tumor progression and immune regulation. Three-dimensional structural analysis characterized regions involved in substrate recognition and catalytic activity and identified an accessible active-site region suitable for ligand targeting. Structure-guided molecular design generated candidate antagonists targeting the TDO2 active site. Compounds were prioritized based on predicted binding interactions, inhibitory potential, solubility, and toxicity-related properties for subsequent experimental validation.

Conclusion

Integrated transcriptomic and structural analyses identified TDO2 as a shared metabolic target across ER+/HER2− and TNBC. Structural characterization revealed a druggable active-site region and supported the design of candidate TDO2 inhibitors. These findings provide a basis for further experimental validation of TDO2-directed therapeutics for breast cancer.
