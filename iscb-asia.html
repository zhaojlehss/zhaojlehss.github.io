Transcriptomic Analysis Identifies TDO2 as a Common Progression-Associated Gene in ER+/HER2− and Triple-Negative Breast Cancer

Jerry Zhao

Introduction

Breast cancer is one of the most common malignancies in women and remains a major cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide. It comprises multiple molecular subtypes, including estrogen receptor-positive/HER2-negative (ER+/HER2−) breast cancer and triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), which differ in tumor biology, therapeutic response, and clinical outcome. Despite this heterogeneity, breast cancer progression and metastasis may involve shared molecular alterations across subtypes. This study tested the hypothesis that genes consistently dysregulated across ER+/HER2− and TNBC tissues may serve as common molecular markers of breast cancer progression and metastatic potential.

Materials and Methods

The publicly available gene-expression dataset GSE58135 was reanalyzed. The dataset included 42 primary TNBC tumors, 42 primary ER+/HER2− tumors, 30 uninvolved breast tissue samples adjacent to ER+/HER2− tumors, 21 uninvolved breast tissue samples adjacent to TNBC tumors, and 5 normal breast tissue samples obtained from reduction mammoplasty procedures in individuals without known breast cancer. Principal component analysis was used to assess global transcriptional differences among sample groups. Tumor and adjacent uninvolved tissues from the same patients were matched, yielding 30 paired ER+/HER2− tumor-adjacent samples and 18 paired TNBC tumor-adjacent samples. Differential expression analyses were performed across tumor, paired adjacent, and normal tissue comparisons. Differentially expressed genes were cross-compared to identify consistently dysregulated candidates. Gene Ontology (GO) analyses were performed to characterize pathways associated with these candidates and their potential roles in tumor progression.

Results

Cross-comparison of differential expression results identified TDO2 as a metabolic marker consistently upregulated across all evaluated comparisons. TDO2 expression was elevated in ER+/HER2− tumors relative to both normal breast tissue and paired adjacent tissue and was similarly increased in TNBC tumors relative to normal tissue and paired adjacent tissue. TDO2 expression was also elevated in uninvolved breast tissue adjacent to both ER+/HER2− and TNBC tumors compared with normal breast tissue from individuals without cancer. TDO2 encodes tryptophan 2,3-dioxygenase, a heme-containing enzyme that catalyzes the first and rate-limiting step of tryptophan catabolism through the kynurenine pathway. GO analysis identified pathways associated with TDO2 dysregulation and suggested a potential role in breast cancer progression and metastasis.

Conclusion

TDO2 was consistently upregulated in both ER+/HER2− and TNBC tumors and was also elevated in uninvolved tissues adjacent to tumors, indicating that TDO2 dysregulation may represent a shared molecular feature associated with breast cancer progression across distinct subtypes. These findings support TDO2 as a potential biomarker of breast cancer progression and metastasis and as a candidate therapeutic target. Further functional and pharmacologic studies are warranted to evaluate TDO2-directed therapeutic strategies in breast cancer.
